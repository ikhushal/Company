What we prepare

Budget estimates
An early view of likely cost, used to set a budget or test whether a scheme is viable before detailed design.
Detailed cost estimates
Measured from drawings and broken down by building element, with labour and materials shown separately.
Material quantity take-offs
Measured quantities of materials for ordering, pricing or checking a supplier’s figures.
Bills of Quantities
Itemised quantities for tendering, so every builder prices the same work and quotes can be compared fairly.
Cost comparisons
Comparing options, such as two layouts or two specifications, or checking received quotes against an estimate.

What we need from you

  • Drawings of the proposed work, or sketches with dimensions
  • Any specification or finishes information
  • The project location, as prices vary by region
  • What is in and out of scope
  • Your intended timescale

If information is missing, the estimate lists the assumptions made in its place.

What you receive

Each estimate is a document you can check, not a single figure. It sets out:

  • the drawings and information it is based on
  • measured quantities, grouped by building element
  • rates and totals for labour and materials
  • assumptions, exclusions and provisional sums
  • items that need input from other professionals

Estimates are supplied as PDF.

An estimate is not a price

An estimate is a reasoned forecast of likely cost, based on the drawings, specification and prices available when it is prepared. It is not a quotation, and it does not guarantee what the work will cost.

Final costs depend on the builder you appoint, market conditions, the final specification and what is found once work starts, particularly in existing buildings. A good estimate makes these uncertainties visible rather than hiding them.

Each estimate lists its exclusions. Typical examples are VAT, professional and statutory fees, and work by others, but the list depends on the project.
