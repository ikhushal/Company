## Deliverable A — Executive project brief

### A.1 What the business appears to offer (supplied)

EverRock Engineering is described as a UK-based, early-stage consultancy offering three groups of pre-construction services:

1. **Cost estimation and cost planning** — building and extension estimates, labour costing, material take-offs, Bills of Quantities, tender estimates, budget planning and cost analysis.
2. **House-extension design** — single- and two-storey, rear, side and wrap-around extensions; garage and loft conversions; internal alterations.
3. **AutoCAD drafting** — existing and proposed plans, site and roof plans, elevations, sections, construction details, as-built drawings, PDF-to-CAD and sketch-to-CAD conversion, and drawing revisions.

All three operate **before construction starts**. EverRock is not a builder or main contractor, and the website must never imply that it is.

### A.2 Likely primary customer groups (assumed)

The brief lists seven groups. They are not equally likely buyers for a new, founder-led practice, and they buy differently:

| Group | Most likely services | How they buy | Launch priority (assumed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homeowners | Extension drawings, extension cost estimate | Infrequent, high-anxiety purchase; need explanation and reassurance; local search | **Primary** |
| Builders and contractors | Take-offs, BOQs, tender estimates, CAD drafting | Repeat, price- and turnaround-sensitive; want scope and format clarity; location matters less | **Primary** |
| Architects and interior designers | CAD drafting, conversions, revisions | Outsourced overflow work; care about drawing standards and file formats | Secondary |
| Developers and investors | Feasibility cost estimates, budget planning | Commercially literate; want assumptions and exclusions shown clearly | Secondary |
| Commercial businesses | Drafting, estimates for fit-outs | Varied; unclear fit | Not targeted at launch |
| Housing associations | Estimates, as-built drawings | Procurement frameworks, insurance and accreditation thresholds | Not targeted at launch |
| Other property organisations | — | Too broad to design for | Not targeted at launch |

Commercial businesses and housing associations can still be served if they enquire. The recommendation is only that the launch site should not be designed around them until evidence supports it. Housing associations in particular usually expect insurance levels, accreditations and procurement documentation that an early-stage practice may not yet hold.

### A.3 Commercial purpose of the website (recommended)

1. Generate **qualified enquiries**: people whose project fits the services, area and capacity on offer.
2. Make the scope and limits of each service clear enough that unsuitable enquiries filter themselves out.
3. Establish credibility for a new practice **without** relying on history, testimonials or a portfolio it does not yet have.
4. Give trade clients (builders, architects) a clear, low-friction way to send work.

### A.4 Main opportunities

- **Drawings and costs together.** Most homeowners obtain drawings from one party and costs from builders' quotes afterwards. A practice that produces both, and keeps them consistent, solves a real problem: discovering after paying for drawings that the design is unaffordable. This is the strongest available differentiator, and it needs no unverifiable claims.
- **Explained, not just listed.** The market for these services is crowded with marketplace freelancers offering "council-ready" drawing packages and take-offs, often with little explanation of scope, responsibility or who is doing the work (Appendix A). A site that states plainly what is included, what is not, what information is needed and what happens next will compare favourably.
- **Honest deliverable samples.** An illustrative drawing set and an excerpt of an estimate, produced by the founder and clearly labelled as illustrative, can show competence before there is a client portfolio.
- **Trade work is less location-bound.** Take-offs and CAD drafting can be delivered remotely. That gives a second revenue line which does not depend on local search.

- The legal entity is unconfirmed. A web search of Companies House records found no company named EverRock Engineering Ltd (Deliverable B, item 1).
- Founder identity, qualifications, memberships and insurance are not supplied.
- Service boundaries are undefined in the areas carrying the most professional risk: planning submissions, Building Regulations drawings and submissions, structural calculations, measured surveys and party wall matters.
- Geographic coverage is unknown.
- There is no portfolio, testimonial, logo or brand asset.
- Pricing model, turnaround expectations and capacity are unknown.
- The telephone number is a placeholder; the domain and mailbox are unverified.

### A.6 Tensions already present in the supplied brief

These are not questionnaire gaps. They are issues within the current material that must be resolved deliberately:

1. **"Engineering" in the company name versus the actual services.** Drafting, estimating and extension design are not engineering design in the professional sense. Visitors may assume structural engineering competence and responsibility. The copy must say precisely what is, and is not, provided.
2. **The working tagline implies construction delivery.** "Building Better Spaces…" reads as a builder's promise, and "Expert" is an unsupported claim. A revised direction is given in Deliverable C.
3. **The audience list is too broad for a founder-led launch.** Designing for seven groups produces a generic site. See A.2.
4. **"Tender estimates" and "Bills of Quantities" carry professional expectations.** In the UK, BOQs are normally prepared to the RICS New Rules of Measurement (NRM). The method of measurement and level of detail must be confirmed before these terms are used.
5. **Saturday hours alongside a placeholder phone number.** Publishing hours implies someone will answer the phone during them. Hours should only appear once the contact arrangements are real.

## Deliverable C — Brand and visual foundation

### C.1 Positioning

**Recommended: drawings and costs, prepared together.**

Lead with the *combination* of design/drafting and cost planning, not any single service.

- **Primary customer attracted:** homeowners planning an extension or conversion who want to understand what it might cost before committing to builders. Secondary: small builders and developers who need drawings and quantities that agree with each other.
- **Benefit communicated:** fewer surprises. The design is drawn with its cost in view, so the client can adjust scope before paying for detailed work or going out to tender.
- **Distinction:** generic drafting services sell drawings, and generic estimating services sell numbers. Few small practices present the link between the two as the point of the service. It is also a claim the business can actually keep, because it describes how the work is done rather than how good it is.
- **Facts that could change it:** if the founder's background is primarily quantity surveying or estimating, Alternative 1 becomes stronger. If extension design excludes planning and Building Regulations work, or the founder cannot visit sites, Alternative 2 becomes stronger. If estimating turns out to be a minor sideline, the integrated story weakens.

**Alternative 1: cost-led ("independent cost planning for residential projects").**
- Attracts: builders, developers and cost-conscious homeowners.
- Benefit: an independent view of cost before tenders and quotes.
- Distinction: residential cost planning is under-served. Quantity surveying practices tend to focus on larger projects, and homeowners usually rely on builders' quotes.
- Changed by: estimating credentials, the cost-data basis (B #24), and whether NRM-standard BOQs are produced.

**Alternative 2: trade support ("drafting and estimating support for builders and design practices").**
- Attracts: contractors, architects and designers outsourcing work.
- Benefit: reliable overflow capacity with clear formats and turnaround.
- Distinction: a named, UK-based, accountable alternative to anonymous marketplaces.
- Changed by: capacity, turnaround commitments, file standards, and whether homeowner work is a priority.

The recommended direction keeps both alternatives available: the services menu is the same, and only the emphasis changes. If the questionnaire points elsewhere, switching costs little.

**Tagline assessment.** "Building Better Spaces Through Expert Design and Cost Planning" is generic, implies construction delivery, and uses "Expert" without evidence. Working replacement:

> **Clear drawings. Better cost decisions.**

It names both halves of the offer and describes an outcome the client controls ("decisions"), not one the business guarantees. Alternatives are given in Deliverable F.

### C.2 Brand personality

| Trait | Means | Does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| Precise | Exact terms, defined deliverables, numbers only when sourced | Jargon-heavy or cold |
| Plain-spoken | Short sentences, practical explanations, limits stated openly | Casual or flippant |
| Measured | Confident about what is offered, restrained about everything else | Hesitant or apologetic |
| Approachable | Writes for a homeowner first, without talking down to a builder | Chatty, emoji, exclamation marks |
| Accountable | A named person, a clear process, a written scope | Corporate "we" hiding who does the work |

### C.3 Tone of voice

- Write like a careful professional explaining a quote across the kitchen table: specific, unhurried, honest about limitations.
- Lead with the client's situation, then the deliverable, then the next step.
- Prefer verbs and nouns to adjectives. Write "We measure your drawings and list every material quantity", not "Comprehensive, accurate take-off solutions".
- Treat estimates as estimates: "an estimate of likely cost, with the assumptions behind it", never "know exactly what you'll pay".
- Refer to regulations with care: "may need planning permission", with a link to the Planning Portal, never "permitted development guaranteed".
- Use sentence case for headings and buttons. It reads more calmly and is the GOV.UK convention British users are familiar with.
- British spelling: specialising, organisation, labour, enquiry, licence (noun) / license (verb), metres.

**Usage rules**

1. Approximate proportions per page: warm white and white 75–80%; stone 10–15%; navy 8–10% (text, header, footer); copper under 2%.
2. **Copper marks the primary action**, with one copper button per conversion area. Secondary buttons are navy outline on light backgrounds. Copper is never used for body links, icons in bulk, or headings.
3. **Links** in body text are navy, underlined, with a thicker underline on hover. They are distinguished by underline, not colour alone.
4. **Navy sections** (footer, final enquiry band) use warm-white text. A primary action on navy uses a warm-white button with navy text (11.64:1), because copper on navy fails contrast.
5. Background alternates between warm white and stone at most. No section uses a third background colour.
6. Status colours are always paired with an icon and text ("Error: …"), never colour alone.
7. No gradients, no transparency effects on text, no gold, no saturated blue, and no yellow/black hazard styling.

**Reasoning.** Navy reads as authority and drawing-office ink. Warm white and stone recall tracing paper and building stone, which keeps the site from feeling corporate-cold. Copper is a real building material, which grounds the accent, and its restricted use keeps calls to action unambiguous.

### C.5 Typography (provisional)

**Recommendation: Inter only, self-hosted, variable font, Latin subset.**

- Inter is designed for screen legibility and is neutral without being anonymous. Its tabular figures (`tnum`) make cost tables align properly, a practical advantage for an estimating business.
- One family keeps the system consistent. A second family is not justified at launch. A monospace for drawing-sheet labels was considered and rejected: it adds weight and complexity for decoration only.
- **Self-hosting** avoids a third-party font request, which is both faster and simpler for privacy.
- **Weights:** 400 (body), 500 (UI, labels), 600 (headings). No 300, no 700+.

- Hairline section dividers with small dimension-line tick ends, used once or twice per page at most.

There is no background grid behind text.

### C.9 Patterns to avoid

- Hero with stock photo of hard-hatted workers, or a CGI house.

### G.3 Privacy, cookies and legal

- **Cookies.** The current ICO guidance page (checked 26 Oct 2026) lists only two exemptions from consent: communication transmission, and storage that is strictly necessary for a service the user requested. It states that revised storage-and-access guidance is under consultation and not yet final. Commentary indicates the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 introduced a narrow statistical-analytics exception with opt-out and information requirements, but this should not be relied on until the ICO's final guidance confirms how it applies. **Recommendation:** launch with no non-essential cookies or similar storage, so no consent banner is needed. Reassess if analytics are wanted.
- **Privacy notice.** Required because the form collects personal data. Must name the controller (the company), purposes, lawful basis, processors (host, email provider), retention period, rights, and the ICO complaint route. Needs the business's confirmation and ideally professional review. A template is not sufficient on its own.
- **ICO data protection fee.** Most organisations processing personal data must pay it unless exempt. Whether EverRock must pay should be confirmed with the ICO's self-assessment.
- **Company disclosures.** Per GOV.UK guidance: registered number, registered office address, part of the UK registered in, and the full name showing limited status. Directors' names and share capital are optional, with conditions if shown.
- **Regulatory content.** Link to the Planning Portal for England and Wales, and to the relevant government sources for Scotland and Northern Ireland if those are served. Never guarantee permitted development, planning approval or Building Regulations compliance.

## Appendix A — Proportional market research (26 October 2026)

Limited desk research, intended only to inform positioning. Observations are separated from assumptions.

**Observed**

- Web searches for UK extension drawing services and UK construction estimating services return predominantly **marketplace freelancer listings** (Fiverr, Cad Crowd) offering "council-ready" planning drawings, Building Regulations packages, structural calculations, take-offs and BOQs. Marketplaces state that they do not vet freelancers' qualifications.
- Local practices listed on directories (Houzz, MyBuilder) typically describe **end-to-end scope** for extensions: site visit, measured survey, drawings, planning application. Example: a Dorset/Hampshire/Wiltshire practice describes handling everything from the initial site visit to submitting the planning application.
- Some UK estimating providers describe nationwide take-off, BOQ and tender-support services. Freelancers name tools such as PlanSwift and Bluebeam.
- UK practice for BOQs is the RICS New Rules of Measurement. On small projects, contractors often measure their own quantities from drawings and schedules of work.
- Current, reliable fee data for small-practice extension drawing packages was not found.

**Inferred (assumptions, not verified)**

- Price competition from overseas and marketplace providers is likely to be intense for standalone drafting and take-offs. A UK-based, named, accountable practice that clearly explains scope and responsibility has a credible way to stand apart.
- Homeowners comparing providers probably struggle to tell which packages include planning submission, Building Regulations drawings or structural calculations. Explicit "included / not included" lists would be a genuine usability advantage.
- A full competitor review of individual practice websites was not performed. If wanted, it should be a short Phase 1 task once the service area is known, focusing on 4–6 practices in that area.
